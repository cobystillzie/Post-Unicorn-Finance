# Emergent Asset Classification — Bottom-Up From 505 Entity Self-Descriptions

_Derived 2026-05-30. Method: one taxonomy-blind research agent per entity (sentence-level verbatim parsing of each firm's own website), 505/505 entities profiled; 12 pattern-finder agents clustered the self-descriptions; an overseer synthesized a candidate taxonomy; an adversarial critic refuted it; a reviser verified counts against `data/evidence/industry_entities.csv` and finalized. Counts are keyword-partition estimates, NOT a verified per-entity reassignment table (that is the required next step before paper use)._

## The six EMERGENT classes (membership differs materially from the prior six)

### PERM-HOLD — Permanent-Hold Operator-Acquirers (buy-and-keep-forever holdcos; software + real-economy sub-axes)  (~115 entities)

**Definition.** Operator-led holding companies that buy whole or controlling equity in already-profitable operating businesses with their OWN or evergreen/permanent capital (not a finite-life LP fund) and explicitly vow never to sell, running them indefinitely as decentralized autonomous units. They define themselves AGAINST the PE buy-to-flip/fund-clock model and sell founders a 'permanent home.' This is the single largest and most coherent emergent cluster in the corpus. It contains two sub-axes that share an IDENTICAL capital signature and differ only by portfolio-company sector: (a) VMS / vertical-market recurring-revenue B2B software acquirers of the explicit Constellation/Topicus/Valsoft lineage and playbook (Aspire, Lumine, Volaris, Banyan, saas.group, Define), and (b) real-economy / Main-Street operating-business acquirers (manufacturing, distribution, trades, services), often with a succession / stewardship / faith-values overlay (Permanent Equity, Garden City, Cranemere, Wrench, Teamshares). They are ONE asset class because the allocator behavior -- raise evergreen/own capital, buy control of profitable companies, never exit, operate decentrally -- is the same; the software-vs-real-economy distinction is a sector tag and the Constellation playbook is a recognizable SUB-CLUSTER, not a separate way of allocating capital.

**Capital signature.** Instrument: full or controlling equity buyout (often all-cash, low/no leverage), funded from evergreen/permanent capital, own balance sheet, family capital, or ESOP -- NOT a time-limited LP fund. Hold-period: permanent / no exit / no fund clock / generational. Target: already-profitable operating companies (software sub-axis: $1M-$10M-revenue mission-critical recurring-revenue B2B/B2G software; real-economy sub-axis: $0.5-5M-EBITDA lower-middle-market / Main-Street businesses, often founder/family-owned facing succession). Operating model: decentralized, autonomous, operate-and-keep.

**Criteria.**
- Acquires whole or controlling equity in operating companies (not minority venture checks, not startups)
- Explicit 'never sell / buy and hold forever / permanent home / not a private equity fund' promise (anti-flip, anti-fund-clock)
- Capital is evergreen/permanent, own balance sheet, family, or ESOP -- not a finite-life LP fund (this is what separates it from finite-hold LMM software PE that contemplates exits)
- Targets already-profitable, cash-flowing businesses (not pre-profit)
- Decentralized / hands-on operate-and-keep structure; buys to build and steward, not to extract
- Sub-axis is a SECTOR tag only: software/VMS (Constellation-lineage playbook) vs real-economy operating businesses -- both share the same allocator behavior

**Common self-description phrases.** permanent home / forever home for [software] companies; buy and hold forever / we never sell / we buy to keep, not to flip; not a private equity fund / no fund clock / no predetermined holding period / no exit; we use our own capital, not a fund / evergreen / permanent capital; we think in decades, not quarters / for generations; decentralized / autonomy to operate; vertical market software / mission-critical software / VSaaS (software sub-axis); steward / stewardship / protect the legacy / the third option (real-economy sub-axis)

**Prototypes.** Aspire Software, Lumine Group, Volaris Group, Banyan Software, saas.group, Define Capital, Chapters Group, Permanent Equity, Cranemere, Wrench Group, Teamshares, Garden City Equity

**Relationship to prior six.** MERGE of two over-split proposals (PHA-SW + PHA-OPS) into one class, and a SPLIT/RESCUE drawn from THREE prior classes. Verified against industry_entities.csv: the software acquirers were scattered across Portfolio Capital (Aspire/Lumine/Volaris/Banyan/saas.group/Everfield/PYXiS/Jonas) and SMV (Define/Big Band/Solen); the real-economy operator-acquirers were overwhelmingly mislabeled Patient Capital (Permanent Equity/Garden City/Cranemere/Wrench/Teamshares/Bay Grove/Enduring -- 12 of 13 checked = Patient, only Chapters = Portfolio). The adversarial critique is correct that PHA-SW vs PHA-OPS shared an IDENTICAL capital_signature and split only on portfolio-company sector, which would proliferate one class per industry -- so they are unified here with sub-axes. Estimated_count is now a DRAW: ~38 Portfolio software-holdcos + ~12 Portfolio ops-holdcos + ~3 SMV software-holdco rescues + ~67 Patient operating-acquirers, capped to the permanent-hold-tagged base (~115), not the inflated 130 the two separate proposals implied.

### SEARCH-ETA — Search-Fund / Entrepreneurship-Through-Acquisition Backers  (~64 entities)

**Definition.** Capital providers whose entire identity is funding an individual 'searcher'/operator-entrepreneur through the two-stage ETA lifecycle (search capital, then acquisition equity) to find, buy, and personally run a single established profitable SME, framed around owner succession. Includes searcher-vehicles, dedicated search-fund sponsors, and fund-of-search-funds layers pooling LP capital across many searchers. The unit of investment is a PERSON plus the one business they acquire. Extraordinarily consistent across the corpus with near-identical vocabulary and a unique two-stage capital structure that no other class shares.

**Capital signature.** Instrument: two-stage equity -- small search capital (often 15-25% of a search cap table) that converts into acquisition equity in a single SME. Hold-period: medium-to-long (3-15y operate-then-exit; some permanent). Target: ONE established, profitable, succession-driven small business, bought and personally operated by the backed entrepreneur as equity-owning CEO. Backer typically takes a board seat and provides mentorship.

**Criteria.**
- Backs an individual searcher/operator-entrepreneur, not a company or a team
- Explicit search-fund / ETA / 'entrepreneurship through acquisition' self-description
- Two-stage capital structure (search then acquisition) is the structural tell -- unique to this class
- Target is a single established profitable SME, framed around succession
- Backed entrepreneur personally operates as CEO post-close (operate-to-own)
- Is an ALLOCATOR of search/acquisition capital -- exclude the surrounding ecosystem (Searchfunder, Search Fund Alliance, Stanford Search Fund Center, EOT Advisors, and advisory-only shops are NOT members)

**Common self-description phrases.** search fund / Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition (ETA); searcher / aspiring CEO / acquisition entrepreneur; find, buy / acquire, and operate / run as CEO; succession / retiring owners / generational transition; by entrepreneurs, for entrepreneurs; fund-of-search-funds; talent leads, capital follows

**Prototypes.** Pacific Lake Partners, Search Fund Partners, Anacapa Partners, Relay Investments, SMEVentures, Brydon Group, Vonzeo Capital, TTCER Partners

**Relationship to prior six.** KEEP essentially as prior 'Search/ETA' -- the single most validated prior category, verified at n=63 with its own unmistakable two-stage vocabulary and capital structure. Minor RESCUE: genuine ETA backers mislabeled into other classes (Brydon Group was tagged Patient Capital) fold back in (+1). Critique-flagged hygiene applied: 'Novastone Capital Advisors' is borderline (advisory framing) and Searchfunder/Search Fund Alliance/Stanford center are confirmed ecosystem non-allocators -- removed as prototypes/members rather than counted.

### NONDIL-GROWTH — Non-Dilutive & Founder-Aligned Growth Capital (anti-VC; revenue-lending + profitable-LMM-equity sub-axes)  (~96 entities)

**Definition.** Capital providers that explicitly position as the founder-friendly alternative to dilutive unicorn VC for ALREADY-REVENUE-GENERATING, capital-efficient companies (mostly B2B SaaS, plus DTC ecommerce, tech-enabled services, and LMM operating businesses), sharing a near-identical 'keep your equity / capital-efficient / no fund-clock pressure / partner not boss' self-description. It contains two instrument sub-axes that DO NOT separate on self-description and so are kept as one class per the project's own self-description-clustering standard: (a) non-dilutive debt / revenue-share -- revenue-based financing, ARR/MRR credit lines, venture debt, shared-earnings agreements, underwritten against the borrower's OWN forward revenue and repaid from cash flow (YouLend, Wayflyer, Lighter, Capchase, Founderpath, Decathlon); and (b) founder-aligned growth equity / flexible-capital LMM PE -- minority or control stakes in profitable bootstrapped companies, often blended with structured credit (mezz/unitranche) and founder-liquidity recaps, contemplating a 3-7y exit (Silversmith, Mainsail, Level Equity, Five Elms, Volition, Serent, LLR, Sunstone). The instruments overlap at the edges (RBF deals carry warrants; growth-equity deals carry blended credit), and the homepages cluster TOGETHER on 'no dilution / capital-efficient / alternative to venture capital,' which is why this is one class with sub-axes rather than two.

**Capital signature.** Instrument: non-dilutive debt or revenue-share / receivables advance / ARR-secured facility (sub-axis a), OR minority/control growth equity blended with structured credit and founder-liquidity recaps (sub-axis b). Hold-period: short revolving to medium (sub-axis a: <3y sales-linked to ~7y term debt; sub-axis b: 3-7y, occasionally to ~15y, with a contemplated exit -- this exit-orientation is what separates the whole class from the never-sell PERM-HOLD holdcos). Target: already-revenue-generating, capital-efficient, bootstrapped B2B SaaS / DTC / tech-enabled / LMM operating businesses. CRITICAL allocator test: must deploy its OWN balance-sheet or fund capital against the borrower/company -- this separates genuine allocators (YouLend 'Our capital'; Lighter Capital) from embedded-finance marketplaces that merely route to partner banks (Levenue, Recur Club, Arc -- excluded as ecosystem).

**Criteria.**
- Positions explicitly as the founder-friendly ALTERNATIVE to dilutive unicorn VC
- Target is already-revenue-generating / profitable / capital-efficient (not pre-revenue startups)
- Deploys its OWN balance-sheet or fund capital (adversarial check: NOT a marketplace routing to third-party lenders)
- Sub-axis a tell: non-dilutive debt or revenue-share underwritten against the borrower's own recurring revenue ('no dilution, no board seats')
- Sub-axis b tell: minority/control growth equity in profitable bootstrapped companies, blended with structured credit / recaps, WITH a contemplated 3-7y exit
- Kept as ONE class because (a) and (b) share self-description vocabulary and overlapping blended instruments -- a self-description clusterer will not separate them

**Common self-description phrases.** non-dilutive / equity-free / no dilution, no board seats; revenue-based financing / repay as a percentage of sales (sub-axis a); credit facility that scales with your ARR / Our capital / we advance against recurring revenue (sub-axis a); growth equity for bootstrapped / profitable / founder-owned companies (sub-axis b); capital-efficient / we partner with founders / founder-friendly; the alternative to venture capital / we are not a typical VC; founder liquidity / take some chips off the table (sub-axis b)

**Prototypes.** YouLend, Wayflyer, Lighter Capital, Capchase, Founderpath, Decathlon Capital Partners, Silversmith Capital Partners, Mainsail Partners, Five Elms Capital, Level Equity, Volition Capital, Serent Capital

**Relationship to prior six.** MERGE forced by arithmetic + the self-description standard. Verified: ALL 10 proposed RBF prototypes AND ALL 10 proposed GE-PROFIT prototypes carry prior_class=SMV, and the SMV pool is exactly 101. Splitting SMV into RBF(55)+GE-PROFIT(55)=110 was arithmetically IMPOSSIBLE from a 101-row pool -- the critique's central finding. The two proposed classes also failed the project's own self-description-clustering test (overlapping 'anti-VC / capital-efficient / no-dilution' vocabulary, overlapping blended instruments). So they collapse into ONE class = the true SMV core (101 minus the ~3 software-holdcos rescued into PERM-HOLD and minus embedded-finance marketplaces purged to ecosystem) ≈ 96. The prior 'SMV' label was simultaneously too narrow (missed permanent-hold software holdcos it wrongly tagged) and too broad (absorbed embedded-finance non-allocators); this class keeps the genuine non-dilutive + founder-aligned-growth core and exports the rest.

### SOV-DEV-BLENDED — Sovereign / Development / Blended-Finance Allocators  (~74 entities)

**Definition.** State, supranational, and development-mandated capital allocators that deploy public or quasi-public capital into companies and funds for strategic, developmental, or catalytic ends rather than pure financial return: sovereign wealth funds, public investment banks, development finance institutions (DFIs), national strategic-capital and innovation agencies, and catalytic/blended/climate-finance vehicles. Their non-unicorn thesis is that they allocate against a national/strategic/developmental mandate with very long or perpetual horizons and a mix of equity, fund-of-funds, and concessional/blended instruments -- a fundamentally different allocator type from any private buy-side class.

**Capital signature.** Instrument: direct equity, fund-of-funds / LP commitments, and concessional/blended/catalytic finance (first-loss, guarantees, climate finance), funded from sovereign or public balance sheets. Hold-period: very long to perpetual; mandate-driven, no fund clock. Target: strategic sectors, national champions, SMEs, deep tech, climate, and emerging-market development -- selected for strategic/developmental fit, not just IRR. Operating model: state/supranational ownership; return objective is blended (financial + strategic/developmental).

**Criteria.**
- Allocates SOVEREIGN, public, or development-mandated capital (sovereign wealth fund, public investment bank, DFI, national innovation/strategic agency, or blended-finance vehicle)
- Deploys against a strategic / developmental / catalytic mandate, not pure financial return
- Instruments include direct equity, fund-of-funds, and/or concessional/blended/first-loss/climate finance
- Very long to perpetual horizon; balance-sheet funded, no LP fund clock
- Is itself an ALLOCATOR (deploys capital into companies/funds) -- exclude pure industry networks like The GIIN (ecosystem)
- Distinct allocator TYPE: state/supranational ownership, not private buy-side

**Common self-description phrases.** sovereign wealth fund / strategic investment / global investment; public investment bank / development finance institution / DFI; blended finance / catalytic capital / first-loss / concessional; national / strategic sectors / innovation / SMEs / deep tech / climate finance; long-term capital for national prosperity / generational mandate; fund-of-funds / cornerstone investor / anchor LP; mobilising private capital / mandate

**Prototypes.** Temasek, GIC, Mubadala, Public Investment Fund, Qatar Investment Authority, Bpifrance, British Business Bank, European Investment Fund, In-Q-Tel, KfW Capital, Khazanah Nasional, NATO Innovation Fund

**Relationship to prior six.** KEEP as prior 'Sovereign Capital' -- the critique correctly flagged that the proposed taxonomy SILENTLY DELETED this class. Verified at n=74 (the corpus's third-largest), of which 42 carry explicit sovereign/DFI/blended-finance vocabulary and the names are unambiguous allocators of state capital (Temasek, GIC, Mubadala, PIF, Bpifrance, EIF, In-Q-Tel). A taxonomy cannot claim to be THIS corpus's emergent structure while dropping its third-largest class; it is restored essentially intact, with the only refinement being removal of any pure industry-network non-allocators (e.g. The GIIN) to the ecosystem layer.

### PATIENT-STEWARD — Patient / Evergreen Stewards (family offices, evergreen LPs, steward-ownership & mission/impact long-hold)  (~60 entities)

**Definition.** The residual, genuine Patient Capital class AFTER the permanent-hold operating-acquirers are carved out into PERM-HOLD: multi-asset family offices, evergreen/perpetual funds, steward-ownership and purpose/EOT-style structures, and mission/impact long-hold investors that allocate their OWN (or aligned) capital across companies and funds with an explicitly patient, no-fund-clock, values- or legacy-driven horizon, WITHOUT themselves directly operating the portfolio companies (which is what distinguishes them from PERM-HOLD operator-acquirers). They hold via minority and control equity and evergreen fund structures and define themselves against the 3-5-year VC/PE exit cycle.

**Capital signature.** Instrument: minority and control equity, evergreen / perpetual fund commitments, and steward-ownership / mission-locked structures; own or aligned long-horizon capital. Hold-period: very long / indefinite / generational, no fund clock -- but the steward does NOT directly run the operating companies (passive-to-active owner, not operator-acquirer). Target: a multi-asset or thematic portfolio of companies and funds selected for long-term compounding, values/impact alignment, or family-legacy fit. Return objective: patient compounding, often with a mission/impact overlay.

**Criteria.**
- Allocates OWN or aligned capital with an explicitly patient / evergreen / perpetual / generational horizon and no fund clock
- Is a family office, evergreen fund, steward-ownership / EOT-style vehicle, or mission/impact long-hold investor
- Does NOT itself operate the portfolio companies (distinguishes from PERM-HOLD operator-acquirers, which run them)
- Holds via minority and/or control equity and evergreen structures across a multi-asset or thematic portfolio
- Defines itself against the short-horizon VC/PE exit cycle
- Watch the Sovereign-overlap trap: impact / mission / global-investment vocabulary is shared with SOV-DEV-BLENDED -- private family-office/impact stewards stay here, state-owned vehicles go to SOV-DEV-BLENDED (e.g. Capricorn Investment Group belongs here, not Sovereign)

**Common self-description phrases.** patient capital / permanent capital / evergreen / perpetual; family office / multi-generational / family-owned / legacy; steward-ownership / purpose / mission-driven / impact; we think in generations / long-term compounding / no exit pressure; aligned, long-term partners / beyond the fund cycle; values-aligned / mission-locked; anchor / cornerstone long-term investor

**Prototypes.** Capricorn Investment Group, Pittsburgh-style family offices, Builders Vision, Emerson Collective, Blue Haven Initiative, Ceniarth, Caprock, Pivotal Ventures

**Relationship to prior six.** SPLIT of prior 'Patient Capital' (the largest prior class, n=175), with the residual explicitly named here per the critique's requirement. The critique correctly showed that the permanent-hold OPERATING-acquirers (Permanent Equity, Garden City, Cranemere, Wrench, Teamshares, Bay Grove, Enduring -- ~67 operating-acquirer-tagged rows) were the bulk of prior Patient and belong in PERM-HOLD. What REMAINS (~56 family-office/impact/evergreen-steward-tagged rows, rounded to ~60 with overlap) is a coherent passive/active long-hold steward class -- this is the true Patient Capital. NOTE: several prototype names here are illustrative archetypes of the residual cluster rather than verified individual rows; the count is a DRAW from the ~56 family-office/impact/evergreen-tagged Patient rows, capped below the ~67 that carved out, so PERM-HOLD(operating) + PATIENT-STEWARD reconcile to the 175 prior Patient pool without double-counting.

### SOLO-MICRO-LMV — Solo-GP / Micro / Indie Funds & Accelerator-Fellowship Backers (LMV)  (~25 entities)

**Definition.** The smallest distinct allocator class: solo-GP and micro funds, indie/rolling funds, and accelerator/fellowship/community vehicles that write very small early checks (and grants/fellowship stipends) into many early-stage founders and bootstrapped builders, explicitly positioned as the lean, founder-proximate alternative to institutional unicorn VC. They allocate real (if small) capital, which keeps them in the atlas, but the surrounding no-capital accelerators and pure communities are ecosystem, not members.

**Capital signature.** Instrument: very small early equity checks, SAFEs, rolling-fund commitments, and fellowship grants/stipends -- own or small-LP capital. Hold-period: long early-stage (typical 7-10y venture horizon), no operating role. Target: many early-stage founders, bootstrapped builders, and indie hackers (often pre- or early-revenue), backed in small amounts with heavy founder proximity / community. Return objective: venture-style upside on a lean cost base.

**Criteria.**
- Writes small early checks / SAFEs / rolling-fund or fellowship capital into many early-stage founders
- Solo-GP, micro fund, indie/rolling fund, or accelerator/fellowship/community vehicle
- Positions as the lean, founder-proximate alternative to institutional unicorn VC (not classic UVC scale)
- Actually DEPLOYS capital (equity or grants) -- no-capital accelerators (e.g. CodeLaunch) are ecosystem, not members
- Distinct from NONDIL-GROWTH: targets early-stage / pre-profit founders, not already-profitable companies
- Smallest class in the corpus but a genuine emergent allocator type

**Common self-description phrases.** solo GP / micro fund / rolling fund / indie fund; we back founders the day-one institutions ignore; fellowship / grant / community / cohort; small checks, high conviction / founder-first; bootstrapped / indie hacker / builder; pre-seed / first check / day-zero capital

**Prototypes.** Weekend Fund, Pioneer, HF0, MicroConf, Forum Ventures, South Park Commons, Freed Fellowship, Fika Ventures

**Relationship to prior six.** KEEP as prior 'LMV' -- verified at n=25, a small but internally coherent solo-GP/micro/indie/accelerator-fellowship class (Weekend Fund, Pioneer, HF0, MicroConf, South Park Commons, Freed Fellowship). It survives the re-cut intact; only hygiene applied is confirming each member deploys capital (equity or grants) and exporting any no-capital accelerators / pure communities to the ecosystem layer.

## Data insights

1. The corpus's single strongest emergent signal is NOT one of the prior six labels -- it is permanent-hold operator-acquirers (buy-and-keep-forever holdcos), ~115 entities, which the prior taxonomy SHATTERED across three classes by keyword: software acquirers landed in Portfolio Capital AND SMV (Aspire/Volaris/Banyan vs Define/Big Band/Solen), while real-economy acquirers were mislabeled Patient Capital (Permanent Equity/Cranemere/Wrench/Teamshares -- 12 of 13 checked = Patient). Reuniting them is the most important correction.

2. The proposed split of permanent-hold into software (PHA-SW) vs real-economy (PHA-OPS) was rejected: both sub-clusters share an IDENTICAL capital signature (evergreen/own capital, control buyout, never-sell, decentralized operate) and differ only by portfolio-company sector. Splitting a capital-allocation taxonomy on portfolio sector would proliferate one class per industry. The Constellation/Topicus/Valsoft lineage is a recognizable PLAYBOOK sub-cluster, not a distinct way of allocating capital -- so it is a sub-axis, not a class.

3. The proposed RBF vs GE-PROFIT split was arithmetically impossible AND failed the project's own self-description standard. Verified directly: all 10 RBF prototypes and all 10 GE-PROFIT prototypes carry prior_class=SMV, and SMV is exactly 101 rows -- you cannot draw 55+55=110 from 101. Beyond the arithmetic, the two share 'anti-VC / capital-efficient / no-dilution / founder-friendly' vocabulary and overlapping blended instruments (RBF deals carry warrants; growth-equity deals carry mezz/unitranche), so a self-description clusterer will not separate them. They are merged into one Non-Dilutive & Founder-Aligned Growth Capital class with an instrument sub-axis.

4. The proposed taxonomy SILENTLY DELETED Sovereign Capital (n=74), the corpus's third-largest class -- a major structural error, not a refinement. The Sovereign pool is unambiguously real (Temasek, GIC, Mubadala, PIF, Bpifrance, EIF, In-Q-Tel; 42/74 carry explicit sovereign/DFI/blended vocabulary). It is restored as Sovereign/Development/Blended-Finance Allocators, a genuinely distinct allocator TYPE (state/supranational ownership, mandate-driven, concessional instruments).

5. Estimated counts in the original proposal were round guesses (55/55/70/60/65), not draws from the corpus. Every count here is re-derived by partitioning the actual 505 rows with keyword tagging so no entity is counted twice: PERM-HOLD ~115, NONDIL-GROWTH ~96, SOV-DEV-BLENDED ~74, SEARCH-ETA ~64, PATIENT-STEWARD ~60, SOLO-MICRO-LMV ~25 (sum ~434, with the remaining ~70 absorbing reassignment slack and ecosystem-removal). Treat all counts as estimates pending the per-entity reassignment table.

6. Patient Capital (n=175, the largest prior class) is really TWO things fused by the word 'patient': permanent-hold OPERATING-acquirers that run the companies (carve out to PERM-HOLD, ~67 operating-tagged) and passive/active long-hold STEWARDS that do not operate them (family offices, evergreen LPs, steward-ownership, mission/impact -- ~56, the true residual Patient). The operator-vs-steward distinction (does the allocator run the portfolio company?) is the clean axis the prior label missed.

7. The Sovereign-vs-Patient keyword overlap (impact / mission / global investment) flagged in CLAUDE.md is now structurally encoded: private family-office/impact stewards stay in PATIENT-STEWARD; state-owned vehicles go to SOV-DEV-BLENDED. Capricorn Investment Group is the canonical example that belongs in Patient, not Sovereign, despite its impact vocabulary.

8. Search/ETA and LMV were the two prior labels that survived the re-cut essentially intact -- Search/ETA because its two-stage search-then-acquisition capital structure is structurally unique and unmistakable, and LMV because the solo-GP/micro/fellowship cluster is small but internally coherent. The prior taxonomy's errors were concentrated in the three keyword-driven labels (SMV, Portfolio, Patient), not in the structurally-defined ones.

9. Six classes is the right number for THIS corpus, but the membership is materially different from the prior six. Net change: keep Search/ETA, LMV, Sovereign; replace Portfolio Capital + the holdco halves of SMV and Patient with PERM-HOLD; replace the rest of SMV with NONDIL-GROWTH; rename the residual Patient as PATIENT-STEWARD. UVC remains the never-promoted baseline, not a class.

10. The cleanup is not done at the taxonomy level: roughly a dozen ecosystem non-allocators are still embedded as members or were proposed as prototypes (Searchfunder, Search Fund Alliance, Stanford Search Fund Center, EOT Advisors, The GIIN, and RBF marketplaces Levenue/Recur Club/Arc that route to partner lenders). Per the allocator test, these must be exported to the Ecosystem/Infrastructure layer before counts are finalized, or the per-class counts will stay ~5-15% inflated by lookalikes.

## Assessment of the prior six-class taxonomy

**Where it works:**
- Search/ETA is the prior taxonomy's strongest, cleanest category -- it emerges intact with a structurally unique two-stage search-then-acquisition capital structure and unmistakable vocabulary; kept essentially as-is (only mislabeled-in rescues like Brydon Group fold back and ecosystem networks are stripped).
- Sovereign Capital is a real, distinct allocator TYPE (sovereign wealth, public investment banks, DFIs, blended/catalytic finance) verified at n=74 with 42 carrying explicit vocabulary -- it must be KEPT, and the proposed taxonomy's omission of it was the single largest error to fix.
- LMV (solo-GP / micro / indie / accelerator-fellowship) is small but internally coherent (n=25) and survives the re-cut intact as its own emergent class.
- The structurally-defined prior labels (Search/ETA, Sovereign, LMV) held up far better than the keyword-defined ones (SMV, Portfolio, Patient), confirming the CLAUDE.md warning that 'classification by keyword count is crude.'

**Where it splits one real category into two (false splits):**
- SMV was split by keyword into revenue-based-financing AND founder-aligned-growth-equity firms that are actually ONE self-described class -- both are already-profitable, capital-efficient, anti-VC, founder-friendly allocators sharing vocabulary and overlapping blended instruments. Re-merged as NONDIL-GROWTH with an instrument sub-axis (the proposed RBF/GE-PROFIT re-split would have re-fragmented it AND was arithmetically impossible from the 101-row SMV pool).
- Permanent-hold SOFTWARE acquirers were split across Portfolio Capital (Aspire/Volaris/Banyan) and SMV (Define/Big Band/Solen) purely by keyword, even though they are one identical buy-and-hold-forever VMS holdco class -- reunited inside PERM-HOLD.
- The proposed taxonomy then RE-committed the splitting error in a new direction by separating permanent-hold software (PHA-SW) from permanent-hold real-economy (PHA-OPS) on portfolio-company SECTOR despite an identical capital signature -- corrected by collapsing them into PERM-HOLD with a sector sub-axis.

**Where it merges two real categories into one (false merges):**
- Patient Capital (n=175) fused two genuinely different allocator types under one word: permanent-hold OPERATING-acquirers that run the companies (Permanent Equity, Cranemere, Wrench, Teamshares -- belong in PERM-HOLD) and passive/active long-hold STEWARDS that merely own them (family offices, evergreen LPs, steward-ownership, mission/impact -- the true residual Patient). Split on the operator-vs-steward axis into PERM-HOLD and PATIENT-STEWARD.
- Portfolio Capital merged permanent-hold software holdcos (Constellation-lineage, ~38 software rows) with non-software operating holdcos (Chapters Group and similar) under a generic 'serial acquirer / holdco' label -- both are really permanent-hold operator-acquirers and fold into PERM-HOLD, so Portfolio Capital as a standalone class dissolves.
- SMV additionally over-merged by absorbing embedded-finance MARKETPLACES (non-allocators that route to partner lenders) alongside genuine balance-sheet revenue-lenders -- the non-allocators are exported to the Ecosystem/Infrastructure layer, tightening the real allocator class.

## Coverage
~88-92% of the 505 genuine atlas entities map cleanly to one of these six classes by capital signature. The high-confidence core is large: Search/ETA (~63), Sovereign/Development/Blended (~74, 42 with explicit vocabulary), and the permanent-hold holdcos (~100+ across former Portfolio + Patient-operating + SMV-software-holdco rows) are unambiguous. The fuzzier ~8-12% sits at three seams: (1) NONDIL-GROWTH's internal RBF-vs-growth-equity boundary, deliberately left as a sub-axis because the homepages do not separate on self-description; (2) the PATIENT-STEWARD vs PERM-HOLD boundary, which turns on the hard-to-read 'does this allocator OPERATE the company or just hold it?' question; and (3) ~10-15 residual ecosystem non-allocators still embedded as members (marketplaces, networks, advisories, embedded-finance routers) that should be removed to the Ecosystem/Infrastructure layer before counts are treated as final. Counts are estimates from keyword-partitioning the 505 rows, not a verified per-entity reassignment table -- that table is the required next step before any paper use.

## Ecosystem non-allocators flagged for removal (allocator test)

- Searchfunder -- search-fund community/directory platform, not a capital allocator (ETA ecosystem)
- Search Fund Alliance -- industry network, not an allocator
- Stanford Search Fund Center -- academic/research center, not an allocator
- EOT Advisors -- advisory shop (employee-ownership transitions), deploys no capital
- Novastone Capital Advisors -- borderline; 'Advisors' framing suggests advisory/syndication rather than balance-sheet allocation -- adversarially re-verify before counting in Search/ETA
- The GIIN (Global Impact Investing Network) -- industry network/standards body, not an allocator (would otherwise be miscounted into Sovereign/Patient on impact vocabulary)
- Levenue -- revenue-based-financing MARKETPLACE that routes to partner funders, not a balance-sheet lender (must NOT sit in NONDIL-GROWTH)
- Recur Club -- RBF/debt marketplace routing to partner lenders, not own-capital allocator
- Arc -- embedded-finance / treasury platform routing to partner banks, not own-capital allocator
- Acquire.com -- startup-acquisition marketplace/broker (for buyers/for sellers, asking price), not an allocator
- Microns -- 'Micro Startups For Sale' marketplace/broker, not an allocator
- CodeLaunch -- accelerator/competition that deploys NO capital, not an allocator
- Purpose US -- steward-ownership advisory/nonprofit, not a capital allocator
- Note on the allocator double-check: 'embedded financing' is NOT automatically ecosystem -- YouLend ('Our capital' + revenue-based funding) and Lighter Capital ARE genuine balance-sheet SMV/RBF allocators and stay in NONDIL-GROWTH. The discriminator is whether the entity advances its OWN capital (allocator) or merely routes to third-party lenders (ecosystem marketplace).
