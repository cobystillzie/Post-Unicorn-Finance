# Exit-Framing Outliers & Case Studies (2026-06-19)

**Capital-innovation / core post-unicorn thesis.** How are people FRAMING startup/company exits? This is a
curated set of **outliers + case studies** around (a) alternative / anti-unicorn exit models (incl. "no exit /
permanent") and (b) the "traditional exit is broken" discourse + what's replacing it.

**Method.** Verified discovery workflow `w3iwnyv76` (84 agents): 69 candidates discovered → adversarially
verified (fetch source + confirm verbatim quote + refute) → 45 survived; a **resume (`w330pq4wc`) then verified
the cut-off candidates, bringing survivors to 66** (additions in §6b). The run hit the session usage limit before
its synthesis step, so this synthesis + the 2-year filter were done by hand from the verified records.

**2-year cap (user directive 2026-06-19): social-listening lookback is capped at 2 years — "two years long and
no more."** Applied strictly ("both must be recent"): a dated post must be **≥ ~2024-06-19**, AND the framing
must be a **current, ongoing phenomenon** (a live operating firm with a current page counts; a defunct model or
a pre-window dated essay does not). What that cut is listed in §7 for transparency.

---

## 1. Bottom line

Within a 2-year window, the **most genuinely NEW exit reframing is "liquidity without exit"** — dedicated funds
and structures that let employees/shareholders cash out *while the company stays private*, reframing the exit as
optional or continuous rather than terminal. This is where the freshest 2025–2026 chatter AND the novel firms
both sit. Around it: the **permanent-hold "never sell" holdco** is loudly marketed as the anti-exit (Permanent
Equity, Tiny, Constellation, Banyan, Mosaic), and smaller threads reframe the *return itself* as **profit-share /
redeemable equity** (Calm/SEAL) or as **buying boring cash flow** (entrepreneurship-through-acquisition). The
older, famous anti-exit narratives (Exit-to-Community, Zebras, Patagonia, DHH) all fall **outside** the 2-year
window.

## 2. Theme A — "Liquidity without exit" (the freshest + most novel) 🟢

Reframes the exit from a single terminal event into recurring/continuous private liquidity. **This is the
standout capital-innovation finding.**

### Dedicated liquidity FIRMS (novel, recent)
**Cyberstarts — $300M Employee Liquidity Fund** · Israel/US · news · first deployed Feb 2026 · **HIGH**
> "This new fund offers structured, annual secondary windows for employees to sell a portion of their vested shares without leaving their company."

A standalone $300M fund whose entire product is recurring annual employee tenders — liquidity exists so people *stay* (a reason **not** to exit). [yahoo/finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cyberstarts-launches-300-million-employee-140500963.html) · *Limit: employee tenders aren't new; "stay private" framing is partly the firm's + a portfolio CEO's.*

**Liquid Stock — "Spark"** · US (NY) · company blog · launched Jan 2026 · **HIGH**
> "Spark is a first-of-its-kind permanent capital solution that provides employees and shareholders with liquidity at a pivotal moment in a private company's life cycle."

Evergreen vehicle advancing up to 50% of share value after a company files its S-1, "without requiring a secondary sale" — cash decoupled from the cap-table event. [liquidstock.com](https://liquidstock.com/blog/liquid-stock-introduces-spark/) · *Limit: crowded pre-IPO-financing field; "first-of-its-kind" is the firm's own claim; still bridges TO an IPO.*

**Collective Liquidity — Collective Exchange Fund** · US · company site · current · **HIGH**
> "Collective Liquidity provides unicorn employees with the financial tools they need to turn their company's equity compensation into a prudent, long-term financial plan."

Imports the public-market **exchange fund** into private markets: swap concentrated private stock tax-free into a diversified evergreen pool and borrow against it — diversified holding replaces the single sale. [collectiveliquidity.com](https://www.collectiveliquidity.com/for-companies) · *Limit: still a route to liquidity; wealth-management-adjacent.*

**Manhattan Venture Partners (Jared Carmel) — "Secondary-as-a-Service™"** · US · current firm (~$1.5B AUM) · **HIGH**
> "My contrarian thesis that venture-backed companies were staying private too long has proven transformative for the industry."

Productized the secondary into the firm's whole model ("Tomorrow's IPOs Today"). [jaredcarmel.com](https://jaredcarmel.com/) · *Limit: self-page; secondaries now consensus; firm predates the window though the model is current; still bridges to an eventual exit.*

### The analyst/discourse half (recent, but now CONSENSUS not outlier)
- **Cardumen Capital — Jorge Rodríguez Ordóñez** (Europe, news): *"Secondary… is the exit path by default right now in venture capital."* — VC must "manufacture liquidity"; companies run "mega-tenders." [globalventuring](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/europe/private-exit-secondary-sales-mega-tenders-sidelining-ipo/)
- **0100 Weekly Brief** (Europe, Substack, Jun 2026): *"Rather than waiting for a single liquidity event at the end of the journey, liquidity can occur throughout a company's life."* [0100conferences](https://0100conferences.substack.com/p/the-ipo-is-no-longer-the-only-exit)
- **CAIA — Koen van Mierlo** (Feb 2026): GP-led continuation vehicles as substitute exit — "structural shift or liquidity patch?" [caia.org](https://caia.org/blog/2026/02/11/continuation-vehicle-boom-structural-shift-or-liquidity-patch)
- **Fabrica Ventures** (2026): *"Venture secondaries are no longer cyclical — they are structural."* [fabricaventures](https://fabricaventures.com/tender-offers-the-new-engine-of-venture-liquidity/)
> These four are the *same consensus narrative* from different mouths — treat as "the conversation," not distinct outliers.

## 3. Theme B — Permanent-hold / "never sell" holdcos (no-exit IS the model) 🟡

An established category, but **actively marketed as the anti-exit** on current sites within the window.

- **Permanent Equity (Brent Beshore)** · US · **HIGH** — *"We invest with no intention of selling (and have never sold a portfolio company)."* 30-year committed funds. [permanentequity.com](https://www.permanentequity.com/) · *Limit: established permanent-capital category; one finder quote was a fabricated composite — this is the on-page verified line.*
- **Tiny (Andrew Wilkinson, TSX: TINY)** · Canada · **HIGH** — *"We do what private equity won't: make fair, straightforward deals, leave your team alone, and hold for the long term."* + *"We're not bargain hunters. We're long-term owners…"* [tiny.com](https://www.tiny.com/) · *Limit: "Berkshire of the internet" is a known model; Tiny itself went public; some finder quotes were unverified (only on-page ones used).*
- **Constellation Software family — Vesta / Jonas / Volaris** · global · **HIGH** — one shared doctrine: *"No company in our family has been, or ever will be, sold"* (Vesta); *"We have never sold a business. Selling to Jonas means you have found a permanent home"* (Jonas); *"After completing hundreds of acquisitions, Volaris Group has never sold a business"* (Volaris). [vesta](https://www.vestasoftwaregroup.com/we-buy-and-hold-forever-what-does-this-mean-for-you/) · [jonas](https://jonassoftware.com/being-acquired) · [volaris](https://www.volarisgroup.com/) · *Dedupe note: these are one Constellation philosophy, not three independent firms.*
- **Banyan Software** · US/global (independent) · **HIGH** — *"Over 100 companies acquired. Zero sold."* "built to be the opposite of private equity." [banyansoftware.com](https://banyansoftware.com/) · *Limit: turns the pledge into a scoreboard, but "zero sold" is trivially true for a young acquirer.*
- **Mosaic Software Group** · US · **HIGH** — *"Mosaic is the best permanent home for ambitious B2B software companies."* Ties it to a "permanent capital base." [mosaicsoftwaregroup](https://www.mosaicsoftwaregroup.com/) · *Limit: founded 2023, no track record; category-standard copy.*

## 4. Theme C — Profit / earnings-share as the return (exit-optional) 🟡

- **Calm Company Fund — Shared Earnings Agreement (SEAL)** · US · **HIGH** — *"A SEAL creates zero pressure for founders to raise further rounds of financing or sell the business, while also keeping those options on the table if the founder chooses."* Return = a capped share of founder earnings. [calmfund.com](https://calmfund.com/shared-earnings-agreement) · *Limit: an RBF variant; fund currently "taking a break"; keeps exit optional, doesn't abolish it.*
- **Redeemable Equity (Innovative Finance Playbook)** · US reference · **HIGH** — founder buyback via a revenue/FCF-linked dividend up to a 2–5x cap, so *the founder repurchasing equity IS the exit*. [playbook.innovative.finance](https://playbook.innovative.finance/chapters/redeemable-equity/) · *Limit: an RBI sub-variant; a reference handbook, not one firm's proprietary thesis.*

## 5. Theme D — Acquisition-as-the-path / ETA (own cash flow, don't chase an exit) 🟡

- **Codie Sanchez / Contrarian Thinking** · US · **HIGH** — *"Many investors overlook these 'boring' businesses, but we think we need more laundromats and window-washing companies, and less AI-driven dating apps."* The mass-market popularizer of buying durable cash-flow businesses instead of the VC startup-and-exit path (book *Main Street Millionaire*, Dec 2024). [codiesanchez.com](https://codiesanchez.com/about/) · *Limit: an ownership philosophy, exit-adjacent; ETA discourse is well-trodden — her edge is reach + explicit anti-tech framing.*

## 6. Theme E — Founder "permanent-home" exit (sell to a holder, not a flip) 🟢

- **Ryan Norbauer — "The Outsider Option: Why I Sold Half my Company to Tiny"** · US · blog · Mar 2025 · **HIGH** — *"I was looking for long-term incentive alignment with smart people rather than a quick payday, and my goals were primarily subjective and psychological."* A seller's-side case study of the permanent-hold model: a 49% minority sale for ongoing profit-share while keeping control. [ryan.norbauer.com](https://ryan.norbauer.com/journal/the-outsider-option-why-i-sold-half-my-company-to-tiny/) · *Limit: one founder's blog; minority-recap mechanics are ordinary, the anti-payday framing is the distinctive part.*

## 9. Plain English

**Bottom line:** Over the last two years, the most genuinely new way people are reframing "the exit" is
**"cash out without selling the company"** — new funds and structures that hand employees and early shareholders
money while the business deliberately stays private. ("**Secondary**" = selling your existing shares to another
investor instead of waiting for the company to be sold or go public; a "**tender**" is a company-organized round
of that.)

The clearest fresh examples are **dedicated liquidity funds**: Cyberstarts raised a **$300M fund just to run
yearly employee cash-outs** (Feb 2026); **Liquid Stock's "Spark"** (Jan 2026) advances pre-IPO employees cash
without a sale; **Collective Liquidity** lets startup employees swap their single risky stock into a diversified
pool tax-free; **Manhattan Venture Partners** turned "the secondary" into its entire business. The shared idea:
liquidity is now something that happens *continuously*, not once at the end.

The other big framing is the **"never sell" holding company** ("**holdco**" = a company that buys and *keeps*
other companies). Firms like **Permanent Equity, Tiny, Constellation's groups (Vesta/Jonas/Volaris), Banyan, and
Mosaic** all market permanence itself as the product — "we will never sell you" — as the opposite of a
private-equity flip. Smaller threads: **Calm Fund's SEAL** and **redeemable equity** pay investors back from
profits so a sale is optional, and **Codie Sanchez** popularizes buying boring, cash-flowing businesses instead
of chasing a startup exit at all.

**What to note:** the famous anti-exit ideas everyone cites — Exit-to-Community, Zebras, Patagonia, "37signals
will never sell" — are all **older than two years**, so by your cap they're context, not current chatter. The
*current* energy is in liquidity-without-exit.
