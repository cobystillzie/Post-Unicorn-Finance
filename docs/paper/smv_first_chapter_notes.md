# SMV First Chapter Notes

## Chapter Claim

Strategic Middle Ventures are not failed unicorns. They are technology-enabled companies built around strategic value, profitable growth, acquisition fit, or durable market position. Their outcomes may be smaller than unicorn outcomes, but their capital needs, risk profile, and liquidity paths are different enough to deserve separate analysis.

## What The Chapter Must Prove

1. There are many companies with meaningful outcomes outside the unicorn path.
2. Strategic acquirers and PE/software platforms already buy these companies.
3. Bootstrapped, delayed-VC, and profitability-first paths can produce valuable exits.
4. The current language of venture often fails to describe these companies accurately.
5. A market intelligence platform can map the category better than ad hoc anecdotes.

## Initial Proof Set Buckets

### Flagship SMV Candidates

- Wingify: bootstrapped Indian SaaS majority acquisition, reported about $200M.
- ProfitWell: SaaS analytics acquired by Paddle for over $200M.
- SocialPilot: reported bootstrapped SaaS acquisition by group.one around $50M.
- FusionCharts: developer-tool acquisition by Idera; bootstrapping claim needs verification.

### Portfolio / Platform-Acquisition Candidates

- Appfire: bootstrapped before growth investment, then acquisition platform in the Atlassian ecosystem.
- Habu: studio/platform-related data collaboration company acquired by LiveRamp for about $200M.

### Boundary Cases

- Mailchimp: too large for SMV, but essential proof that non-VC paths can create massive value.
- Assurance IQ: too large for SMV, but useful as a bootstrapped-to-strategic-acquisition boundary case.
- Cloudinary and Netcore: durable/profitable scale cases, more Patient Capital than SMV.
