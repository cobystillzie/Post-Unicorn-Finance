# Taxonomy

## Classification Rule

Only call a category an **asset class** if it has a distinct:

- return model,
- underwriting logic,
- capital structure,
- risk profile,
- liquidity path,
- and investor/founder fit.

If a category does not meet that standard, classify it more precisely as a capital strategy, funding instrument, operating philosophy, ownership model, company archetype, liquidity pathway, source of capital, or ecosystem structure.

## Primary Categories

### UVC: Unicorn Venture Capital

Traditional venture capital optimized for power-law returns and billion-dollar outcomes.

Traits:

- hypergrowth,
- massive TAM,
- multiple funding rounds,
- high burn tolerance,
- IPO or mega-acquisition path,
- returns driven by a few massive winners.

Status: established asset class.

### SMV: Strategic Middle Ventures

Tech-enabled companies with strategic acquisition, profitable-growth, or middle-outcome logic.

Traits:

- strategic acquisition fit,
- vertical or workflow depth,
- capital-efficient growth,
- proprietary data, specialized IP, customer depth, or integration value,
- meaningful outcomes that may not require unicorn scale.

Status: emerging asset-class candidate. This is the first proof lane.

### Patient Capital

Long-duration ownership for companies designed to compound over time without requiring a sale.

Traits:

- durable profitability,
- cash-flow orientation,
- dividends, profit-sharing, royalties, or permanent equity,
- no forced exit timeline,
- founder control can matter more than speed.

Status: asset class or capital strategy depending on vehicle structure.

### LMV: Leveraged Micro Ventures

Small AI/software-enabled teams creating outsized value with low capital needs.

Traits:

- 1-10 person teams,
- automation-heavy,
- low burn,
- high revenue per employee,
- small checks or no institutional capital,
- outcome size can be meaningful despite tiny teams.

Status: emerging AI-era asset-class candidate.

### Sovereign Capital

Innovation capital deployed for national, regional, or strategic-interest reasons.

Traits:

- government or sovereign wealth involvement,
- long time horizons,
- strategic sectors such as AI, defense, energy, semiconductors, biotech, climate, food security, and infrastructure,
- return objectives may include resilience, jobs, competitiveness, or security.

Status: source-of-capital class and strategic allocator logic, not always a startup asset class.

### Search / ETA Capital

Entrepreneurship through acquisition: buying and operating an existing business instead of founding one.

Traits:

- operator-centric acquisition,
- existing revenue and customers,
- debt/equity/search-fund structures,
- lower existential risk than startups,
- operational execution is the main risk.

Status: established adjacent asset class; distinct from VC and traditional PE because the operator is central.

### Camel Capital

Capital-efficient, resilient, profitability-oriented company building.

Traits:

- limited rounds,
- lower burn,
- faster path to profitability,
- survival before scale,
- less dependence on hype cycles.

Status: modifier or operating philosophy, not a standalone asset class unless research proves a distinct return model.

### Portfolio Capital

Venture studios, foundries, holding companies, or repeatable company-creation/acquisition platforms.

Traits:

- shared infrastructure,
- repeatable creation or acquisition,
- shared operators, engineering, finance, distribution, recruiting,
- portfolio itself may become the operating engine.

Status: operating model by default. It becomes an asset-class candidate only when investors underwrite the platform rather than single companies.

## Classification Outputs

Every database row should answer:

1. What entity, instrument, or event is being claimed?
2. What source supports it?
3. Which pathway does it suggest?
4. Is the classification verified fact, candidate evidence, inference, or user thesis?
5. Why does this help prove Post-Unicorn Finance exists?

The atlas-first tables classify industry entities before company examples. `company_events.csv` remains supporting evidence for SMV and case-study chapters.
