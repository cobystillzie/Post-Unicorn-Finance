# State of Value Participation in Startups — Baseline (2026-06-18)

The baseline Ethan asked for: **is meaningful employee value participation (ESOPs / broad equity /
profit-sharing) becoming a trend across startups, and where is the conversation in new PE/VC?** Synthesized
from a 12-agent source-cited research + social-listening sweep (trend data, examples, PE/VC thought
leadership, and Reddit / Hacker News / LinkedIn / X / blogs). Posture: balanced; rising/flat/unclear called
honestly. **Sourcing caveats are in §7 — read them before quoting.**

---

## 1. Bottom line

**There is no single "yes, it's a trend."** The honest answer is three separate movements going in different
directions:

1. **Employee ownership in the true sense (a trust that makes workers owners) is NOT a startup trend.** It is
   structurally a *mature-company / private-equity* phenomenon, rare in venture-stage startups.
2. **In startups, conventional equity is seen as broken and its per-grant value has declined** — and
   rank-and-file sentiment is deeply skeptical (persistent, not new).
3. **What IS rising in startups: liquidity** — the mechanisms that let employees actually *cash out* equity
   (tender offers, secondaries, longer exercise windows) — plus a smaller but growing **"equity for all"**
   broad-based movement, strongest in Europe.

The loudest, fastest-growing *conversation* about worker value participation is in **new PE (Ownership
Works / KKR)**, not VC. And a distinct **"you don't need a unicorn / keep your equity"** thread among
operators ties directly into the Post-Unicorn thesis.

## 2. The terminology trap (resolve this first)

"ESOP" means two unrelated things, and conflating them produces wrong conclusions:
- **NCEO / ERISA sense — Employee Stock Ownership *Plan*:** a trust that holds company stock for employees
  (retirement-plan law). Makes workers real owners. ~6,609 plans, 15.1M participants, >$2T assets (NCEO).
- **Startup / VC sense — Employee Stock Option *Pool*:** a reserve of options set aside for employees
  (~10–16% of the cap table). Not ownership — a hiring currency.

**True employee-ownership ESOPs are rare in startups by structure** — they need a mature, profitable company
with stable cash flow, the opposite of a venture-stage startup (source: NCEO; ESOP feasibility guidance).
So when someone asks "are startups doing ESOPs?", the honest answer is: *not the ownership kind — that's a
mature-company/PE thing.* (NCEO: https://www.nceo.org/articles/employee-ownership-by-the-numbers)

## 3. What is actually happening *in startups* (the data)

**Per-grant equity value is down and has stayed down** (Carta, paraphrased — see §7):
- New-hire equity packages fell ~26–37% from the late-2022 peak; ~26% below pre-2022 levels in 2024–25.
- Employees increasingly prefer cash; option-*exercise* rates fell from ~58% (2021) to ~30% (2023).
- Exception: **AI/ML engineer** grants rose ~31% (Jan-2024→Feb-2026). (https://carta.com/data/equity-compensation-2024/)

**Liquidity access is rising — the clearest positive trend:**
- **Tender-offer transaction value +68% YoY (Q2 2025)** on Carta; median participation rate rose from 36.6% (Q1 2021) to 56% (H1 2025); ~61% of 2025 tenders were Series C+ (Carta, verbatim).
- **Extended post-termination exercise windows roughly doubled** (~5%→11% of employees over ~4 yrs).
- **VC secondaries ($61.1B) overtook VC-backed IPO value ($58.8B)** (Jul-2024→Jun-2025).
- (Carta: https://carta.com/data/tender-offers-q2-2025/ ; https://carta.com/data/vc-secondary-trends-q2-2025/ ;
  Nasdaq Private Market tender volume ~5× in two years: https://www.nasdaqprivatemarket.com/secondary-scene-npm-annual-private-market-report/)

**Option-pool norms** (Index Ventures "Rewarding Talent" + Carta): recommended ~12% at Series A rising to
~16% by Series C; *measured* pools smaller (median seed ~11.8%). Individual grant sizes declining since 2021–22.
(https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/)

**Broad "equity for all" — small but rising, Europe-led:** examples include Buffer (15% of net profit to all
employees, page-verified), SafetyWing, Checkly. A widely-cited claim that **58% of UK companies now grant
equity to all employees (+16% YoY)** traces to **Ravio's 2026 Compensation Trends report** — cite it *as attributed to Ravio* (a single-vendor benchmark), not as established fact.
(Buffer: https://ravio.com/blog/compensation-examples-buffer)

## 4. Where the conversation sits in new PE / VC

**New PE — loudest and concretely growing (but now contested):**
- **Ownership Works** (founded by KKR's Pete Stavros): 184 companies with shared ownership, 268,000
  non-C-suite employees, $14B total expected value, goal of **$20B for workers by 2030**. (https://ownershipworks.org/our-impact/)
- **KKR's FY2025 10-K:** 80+ current/former portfolio companies have awarded billions in equity to 180,000+
  non-senior employees. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001404912/000140491226000007/kkr-20251231.htm)
- **Apis & Heritage** held an $85M first close on Legacy Fund II (2025-06-24; $250M target). (https://www.apisheritage.com/lf2-first-close)
- Note: this movement is increasingly *debated*, not uncritically praised — and it is **PE (mature companies),
  not startups.**

**VC — following / largely absent on broad ownership (now verified).** An adversarial 6-angle sweep (44 agents,
14 confirmed findings, 2026-06-18) found **no VC fund with a broad-employee-ownership thesis** — no VC analog to
Ownership Works. The most current VC voice (Bijan Sabet, Spark Capital, Apr 2026) treats fair employee equity as
**table-stakes mechanics** (*"I don't think any of this is radical"* — his point is fairness *should* be
unremarkable, not that ambition is capped); older, longstanding voices agree (Hunter Walk, 2020, expected broad
value-sharing from *"non-venture backed companies"*; Fred Wilson questioned broad grants, 2018). Where venture
money touches employee ownership it is **picks-and-shovels** — funding startups (Teamshares $245M from
QED/USV/Khosla; Common Trust $2.6M seed) that convert *other* mature/small businesses to employee ownership, not
broad ownership at venture-backed startups themselves. Academia (Rutgers, Aspen) is actively trying to pull VC
in. Net: **PE leads; VC follows/absent.** *(Moderate confidence: searched-not-found, not proven absence; see §7.)*

**Operators / fund blogs — a directly relevant rising theme:** "you don't need a unicorn / keep your equity /
sub-unicorn liquidity" essays are spreading, alongside "traditional startup equity is broken" and "value
participation is migrating to secondaries." (e.g. https://thefundcfo.substack.com/p/338-you-dont-need-a-unicorn-your ;
https://criscafiero.substack.com/p/golden-handcuffs-the-startup-equity)

## 5. Social-listening read (employee sentiment)

Across **Hacker News, Reddit, X** the dominant rank-and-file view is **deep, persistent skepticism** of
conventional startup equity — "options are worth $0 until sold," diluted away, behind liquidation
preferences, hit by tax traps (AMT) and 7–10-year illiquidity. This skepticism is **flat/persistent, not
rising** — it's the long-standing baseline. (HN "Startup Equity 101", Jun 2025: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44188178)
On **LinkedIn**, by contrast, the broad-ownership / value-participation *theme* is **rising** (moderate
confidence), split between a startup/VC option-sizing community and a PE/Ownership-Works community.

## 6. Why this matters for Post-Unicorn Finance

- The "**equity is broken + liquidity is migrating to secondaries**" reality is the *same liquidity problem*
  your atlas funds solve from the capital side — value participation and post-unicorn liquidity are two ends
  of one rope.
- The "**keep your equity / don't chase a unicorn**" operator movement is your thesis showing up in the
  worker-equity conversation — a natural bridge, and a place the project could plant a flag.
- The honest baseline ("**true employee ownership is a PE/mature-company thing, not yet a startup trend**")
  is itself the finding Ethan wanted, and it sets up the *next* question: is post-unicorn capital positioned
  to be the vehicle that finally brings real value participation to startups?
