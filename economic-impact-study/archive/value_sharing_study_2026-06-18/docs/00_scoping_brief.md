# Scoping Brief — Per-Dollar Impact of Post-Unicorn Capital on Worker Value-Sharing

**Study:** Economic Impact of Post-Unicorn Capital Innovation
**Commissioned by:** Ethan (thesis owner), 2026-06-17
**Prepared:** 2026-06-17 · **Status:** Phase 0 (scoping + pilot) · **Audience:** academic-grade (citable; shareable with the professor)
**Posture:** balanced, evidence-graded, never overclaimed.

---

## Abstract

The Post-Unicorn Finance project has catalogued capital allocators operating outside classic unicorn
venture capital. This study opens a new, orthogonal line of inquiry: **the value-creation layer.** Not how
companies are *funded* (inputs) or how investors *exit* (outputs), but **how the value a company creates is
shared with the people who build it — and whether that sharing changes the odds the company succeeds.**

The study has two equal halves. The **descriptive** half maps the landscape of worker value-sharing
mechanisms (ESOPs, profit-sharing, warrants/options, phantom equity, employee-ownership trusts,
cooperatives, steward-ownership, and emerging models). The **analytical** half tests a single comparative
thesis: *does the post-unicorn capital model deliver more worker value-sharing — and better-distributed
economic impact, per dollar deployed — than the unicorn-chasing venture-capital model?* The analytical half
combines (a) a graded synthesis of existing empirical evidence and (b) an original, normalized
post-unicorn-vs-VC comparison **at the fund level** (rows = funds; portfolio companies roll up), piloted in
Phase 0 across 13 funds. *(Corrected 2026-06-18: the first pilot wrongly used companies as the unit — see
`../analysis/2026-06-18_audit_and_rebuild.md`.)*

The central methodological contribution is a **fairness engine** that makes the two capital stacks
comparable despite vast differences in scale, timing, and survivorship — by measuring impact **per dollar
deployed** and by **distribution share** (who captures the value), while still reporting absolute impact
honestly.

## 1. Background and motivation

The study originates in a question Ethan could not answer when a professor asked whether capital innovation
extends into **ESOPs, employee participation, warrants, and profit participation** — and "possibly even
terms I do not know." Ethan classified the topic as tangential to the core thesis: *"it has nothing to do
with the inputs (how companies are funding) or outputs (liquidity events) but rather how value is created
and parameters on tipping the odds that something will work."*

That phrasing defines the study's position precisely. The project's existing **INPUT→OUTPUT model** sorts
funds by what they fund and the liquidity outcome they target (Traditional VC: large markets → unicorn in
7–10y; SMV: large-ish markets → sub-unicorn liquidity; Nimble: smaller markets → 3–5y liquidity). This
study addresses a **third axis that the input/output model is silent on: the distribution of created value
and its effect on success.**

Two facts about the existing project shape the scope:

- **Steward-ownership already lives inside the atlas** (as a feature of Patient Capital). This study is
  therefore a new *lens*, not a new asset class. Forcing employee value-sharing into the taxonomy would
  violate the project's "classification credibility is everything" rule.
- **The atlas is the natural test population.** The ~123 verified post-unicorn allocators (and, in later
  phases, their portfolio/owned companies) are exactly the sample needed to ask whether post-unicorn
  capital also innovates on value-sharing — without classifying value-sharing itself.

## 2. Research questions

**Descriptive (the map).**
- D1. What mechanisms exist for sharing a company's created value with its workers?
- D2. How does each work — legally, financially, and in practice — and where (US vs international)?
- D3. Which mechanisms are *emerging* or unfamiliar to generalist investors ("terms I don't know")?

**Analytical (the claim).** Decomposed into the chain Ethan specified (allocator → company → outcomes):
- A1. **Does the capital model drive value-sharing?** Are post-unicorn-backed firms more likely to share
  value broadly than VC-backed firms? *(capital → sharing)*
- A2. **Does value-sharing tip the odds?** Does broad worker value-sharing improve firm and worker outcomes?
  *(sharing → outcomes)* — answered primarily through graded synthesis of existing evidence.
- A3. **Net comparison.** Per dollar deployed, and by distribution of that value, does the post-unicorn model
  produce more and better-distributed economic impact than the unicorn-chasing model? *(the full chain)*

## 3. Scope and definitions

- **Worker value-sharing:** any structure that gives non-executive employees a claim on the value they help
  create — ownership (equity, ESOP, EOT, co-op, steward-ownership), upside instruments (options, warrants,
  phantom equity, SARs), or cash participation (profit-sharing). Full catalog: `02_mechanism_catalog.md`.
- **The two capital stacks:**
  - *Unicorn-chasing VC* — classic venture capital pursuing power-law, $1B+ outcomes via concentrated equity.
  - *Post-unicorn capital* — the atlas universe (SMV, Permanent/Patient, Studio, Nimble, Impact, etc.):
    allocators pursuing durable, sub-unicorn, or permanent outcomes.
- **Geography:** US-primary (ESOPs, profit-sharing, equity comp), with UK (EOTs), Germany/Netherlands
  (steward-ownership), and Spain/Italy/France (co-ops) as comparison and as a source of unfamiliar models.
- **Success / "works" (dependent construct):** reported as **multiple separate outcomes** rather than one
  blended definition, with **"value actually reaches workers"** treated as a central outcome in its own right
  (per the thesis owner's instruction).

## 4. Study design

- **Unit of analysis:** the **full chain** — allocator model → company value-sharing practices → worker and
  economic outcomes — examined at the fund level, the company level, and as a linked path.
- **Descriptive half:** a broad-and-deep, source-cited catalog (`02_mechanism_catalog.md`), explicitly
  flagging mechanisms likely unfamiliar to a generalist VC.
- **Analytical half:**
  1. **Evidence synthesis** (`03_evidence_synthesis.md`): grade what is already established about A2, honestly
     separating causal identification from correlation and independent research from advocacy.
  2. **Original comparison** (this study's novel contribution): the normalized post-unicorn-vs-VC analysis,
     piloted now and scaled in Phase 2.

## 5. The comparison methodology (summary)

Full specification in `01_comparison_methodology.md`. The core problem is that the two stacks are not
naïvely comparable: a single unicorn can create thousands of jobs with billions of dollars; a small SMV
fund cannot match that in absolute terms, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The fairness engine
resolves this with three normalizations and one honesty rule:

1. **Per-dollar deployed** — impact per $1M of capital, using **capital deployed across the whole fund
   portfolio** (including failures) as the denominator, not just survivors.
2. **Distribution share** — what fraction of created/realized value reaches non-executive workers vs.
   founders and investors.
3. **Per-year (annualized)** — to neutralize the fact that unicorn exits arrive years later than
   post-unicorn liquidity (with the timing difference also stated as a caveat).
4. **Honesty rule** — absolute impact (the unicorns' real scale) is *also* reported, so both truths stand.

Confounders are handled through a **nine-difference framework** (scale, survivorship, exit timing, build-vs-buy,
value distribution, paper-vs-realized wealth, sector/geography, spillovers, additionality) detailed in `01`.

## 6. Outcomes and metrics

Reported separately (never collapsed into a single index):

| Outcome | Pilot proxy (public-data) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Worker ownership depth | % of equity/upside held broadly by employees; breadth of participation | The core mediator |
| Jobs & job quality | headcount; jobs **created** vs **preserved** (labeled); wage/retention signals where public | Build-vs-buy distinction enforced |
| Who captures the upside | distribution of realized value: worker share vs founder/investor share | The distribution lens |
| Survival & longevity | firm age, survival, continuity of ownership | Strongest in the existing literature |

## 7. The causal half (does value-sharing tip the odds?)

Answered primarily by **grading existing evidence**, not by running our own econometrics in Phase 0. The
relevant bodies of work (graded in `03_evidence_synthesis.md`): the Rutgers/NBER "shared capitalism"
program (Kruse, Blasi, Freeman); NCEO and ESOP performance/survival studies; the UK Ownership Effect Inquiry
and EOT evidence; and the worker-cooperative survival literature (Mondragón, Italy, France). The central
threat — **selection bias / endogeneity** (the kind of firm that adopts broad ownership may already be
healthier) — is treated as the primary thing to confront, not gloss over.

## 8. Data strategy

Per the 2026-06-17 directive, the Phase-0 pilot uses **public / compliant sources only** — company filings,
SEC EDGAR, DOL Form 5500 (ESOP filings), NCEO datasets, official reports, and company disclosures. **No
ToS-violating scraping** (consistent with the project rule that social platforms are discovery-only and site
protections are not bypassed). A full **metric × data-provider × cost matrix** is compiled in
`04_data_strategy.md` so that licensed providers (e.g., commercial firm-data vendors) can be integrated
*after* the first 20-vs-20 is reviewed and greenlit by Ethan.

## 9. Pilot (Phase 0): fund-level comparison

A small, deliberately deep **fund-level** pilot: **13 funds** — 5 atlas allocators + 3 worker-ownership
allocators (Teamshares, Apis & Heritage, Mosaic) vs 5 matched VC funds — with each fund's portfolio
(a documented sample) rolled up. For each portfolio company we capture the two metrics that matter here:
**employee-participation / ESOP presence** and **headcount (jobs)**. Every datapoint is source-cited; missing
values are "not public" rather than estimated; each fund passed an adversarial verify.

**Headline (caveated):** broad employee participation by group — worker-ownership 26/26, atlas 1/28, VC 0/39.
Worker value-sharing is real but currently lives in a *small dedicated niche*; it has **not** diffused into
the broader atlas/post-unicorn capital. ("Unknown" dominates the atlas sample — thin data, not proven
absence; no per-dollar comparison is asserted.) Datasets: `../data/funds_pilot.csv` (fund rollup) and
`../data/fund_portfolio_companies.csv`; first cuts: `../analysis/fund_level_first_cuts.md`.

## 11. Limitations and threats to validity

- **Selection bias / endogeneity** (A2): firms choosing broad ownership may differ systematically. We lean on
  the most causally identified studies and flag the rest as correlational.
- **Ecological inference** (Phase 3): country-level correlations cannot establish firm-level causation;
  hence the country/wellbeing link is explicitly deferred and never asserted in Phase 0.
- **Public-data coverage gaps:** private-company ownership, cap-table, and wage data are often undisclosed;
  the pilot will be honest about what is missing (this is itself a finding motivating licensed data).
- **Additionality / counterfactual:** whether a firm would have existed or grown without a given capital
  source is generally unobservable; we do not claim causal additionality.
- **Definition heterogeneity:** "employee ownership" spans 1%-option-pools to 100%-worker-co-ops; we
  disaggregate rather than average.

## 12. What this study does NOT claim

- It does **not** assert that worker value-sharing *causes* success on the strength of correlational data.
- It does **not** claim a country's happiness is caused by its capital model (Phase 3 is designed, not run).
- It does **not** treat value-sharing as a new atlas asset class.
- It does **not** use any data obtained by bypassing site terms of service.
