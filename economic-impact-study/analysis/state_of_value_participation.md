# State of Value Participation in Startups — Baseline (canonical, living doc)

**The question Ethan asked:** before comparing funds, first establish — *is meaningful employee value
participation (ESOPs / employee participation / broad equity / profit-share) becoming a trend across
startups, and where is the conversation in new PE and VC?* SpaceX is the concept example but **not a
startup** — the test is whether *actual startups* are adopting this and whether it is rising.

This is the single consolidated baseline (merges the prior report + the Exa and credentialed social-listening
pulls; the dated source files are archived under `../archive/baseline_sources_2026-06-19/`). **Read §9
caveats before quoting.** Pairs with the parked SMV-vs-unicorn externalities methodology

## 1. Bottom line

**There is no single "yes, it's a trend."** Three movements run in different directions:

1. **True employee *ownership* (a trust/plan that makes workers owners) is NOT a startup trend** — it is
   structurally a *mature-company / business-succession / PE* phenomenon, and it is genuinely accelerating
   *there*.
2. **Conventional startup equity is distrusted and its per-grant value has fallen** — employee sentiment is
   deeply, persistently skeptical.
3. **What IS clearly rising at startups is *liquidity*** (tender offers, secondaries) — the ability to cash
   out existing equity — capped by the **SpaceX IPO (June 12, 2026)**, plus a smaller Europe-led "equity for
   all" movement.

The loudest, fastest-growing *conversation* about worker value participation is in **new PE (Ownership
Works / KKR)** and **policy**, not VC. **VC is largely absent** on broad worker ownership. A distinct
operator thread — *"you don't need a unicorn / keep your equity"* — is the natural bridge to the
Post-Unicorn thesis.

## 2. Terminology trap (resolve first)

"ESOP" means two unrelated things:
- **Ownership *Plan* (NCEO/ERISA sense):** a trust holding company stock for employees; makes them real
  owners. ~6,600 plans, ~15M participants, >$2T assets. Mature/profitable companies.
- **Startup option *Pool*:** a reserve of options (~10–16% of the cap table); a hiring currency, **not
  ownership.**

**True employee-ownership ESOPs are rare in startups by structure** — they need stable cash flow, the
opposite of a venture-stage startup (NCEO). So "are startups doing ESOPs?" → *not the ownership kind.*

## 3. What is actually happening *in startups* (Carta, primary-verified 2026-06-18)

- **New-hire equity packages −37%** (Nov 2022 → Jan 2024), verbatim; ~26% below pre-2022 levels in 2024-25.
- **Option-exercise rate fell 58% → 30%** (Nov 2021 → Nov 2023) — employees increasingly don't even exercise.
- Exception: **AI/ML engineer** grants rose ~31%.
- Option-pool norms: ~12% at Series A rising to ~16% by Series C (Index Ventures); individual grants shrinking.
  (carta.com/data/equity-compensation-2024 ; indexventures.com/rewarding-talent)

## 4. Employee OWNERSHIP — real and accelerating, but mature-company / succession / PE (not startups)

- **DOL/EBSA: "becoming mainstream"** — **+28% in leveraged standalone ESOPs**; EOTs and co-ops "taking
  hold"; worker co-ops up ~3.5× in a decade (~820). ESOPs used as a *succession* tool for mid-size private
  firms + retirement plans at public corps. (rethinking65.com, 2026-03-05)
- **Aspen Institute (2026-05-29):** ~**18% of US employees (~25M)** have some ownership stake; ~11M in ESOPs.
- **"Quiet Takeoff" (AInvest, 2026-04-07):** since 2022, **>$1.3B distributed to 41,000 non-exec employees**,
  projected **>$20B in the coming decade**; UN 2025 International Year of Cooperatives.
- **Financing is scaling (ImpactAlpha, 2025-08-06):** EO-transition financiers grew "from a small handful of
  funds to **more than three dozen active managers**"; Rutgers: ~140,000 businesses / 33M employees
  *eligible* to convert; **Apis & Heritage** closed **$85M** on Fund II ($250M target). Stavros: *"The
  problem with employee ownership is it's really hard… It takes years."*
- **Fresh example:** P. Terry's (Austin, ~1,800 staff) → **Employee Ownership Trust**, profit-share 5%→20%,
  advised by **Common Trust**; chosen *instead of* selling to PE. (Statesman, 2026-06-09)
- Institutional: **Norges Bank** (largest SWF) endorses employee share ownership; **Walmart** expanding
  employee equity.

## 5. Liquidity — the clearest rising trend (now incl. the SpaceX IPO)

- **SpaceX IPO (Fri June 12, 2026) → ~4,400 employee millionaires** — "one of the largest corporate wealth
  creation events in history" (X: @Intellectualins, @SebastinPatron3, 2026-06-13; planning context
  creativeplanning.com, 2026-06-17). Ethan's concept example, now a realized liquidity event.
- **Tender-offer volume:** $3.8B (2023) → **$16.5B (2025)**; ~**16,538 employees** in tenders in 2025
  (record). US tenders ~**$18.4B in 2025** (PitchBook). (Carta/Peter Walker, 2026-04-07)
- **Secondaries > IPOs:** VC secondaries **$61.1B** > VC-backed IPOs **$58.8B** (Jul 2024–Jun 2025, Carta
  verbatim); **>$100B** of private shares changed hands via secondaries in 2025 (Fabrica, 2026-06-14).
  Tender value +68% YoY (Q2 2025, Carta).
- **OpenAI Oct-2025 secondary:** 600+ employees sold ~$6.6B at $500B (up to $30M each) — *and* "record
  holdouts" (Anthropic employees electing to hold). (Earlyasset, 2026-06-15)
- The tender is becoming a recurring **compensation instrument** (OpenAI, Stripe, Anthropic), and startups
  now compete on it vs OpenAI/SpaceX. (augment-pulse, 2026-05-28; TechCrunch, 2026-02-05)
- **Mastercard launched a global ESPP** (Morgan Stanley at Work) for "broad-based employee participation" (X:
  @johntoomeyMS, 2026-06-18) — public-company broad-equity expansion.

## 6. Where the conversation sits — PE/policy lead, VC absent

- **New PE — loudest, concretely growing:** **Ownership Works** (KKR's Pete Stavros) — 184 companies, 268,000
  non-exec employees, **$20B-for-workers-by-2030** goal. (ownershipworks.org)
- **Policy momentum:** **Rhode Island Senate unanimously passed** a bill to create a State Employee Ownership
  Center (X: @theNCEO, 2026-06-18); bipartisan federal/state activity.
- **VC — largely ABSENT** (verified adversarial sweep, 14 confirmed / 22 weak findings): the most sympathetic
  VC voice (Bijan Sabet, Spark) caps ambition at fairer *options* — *"I don't think any of this is radical."*
  Hunter Walk (Homebrew): broad value-sharing is *"more likely the non-venture backed companies."* **No VC
  has a portfolio-wide broad-employee-ownership thesis** analogous to Ownership Works.
- **The one VC touchpoint = picks-and-shovels:** VC-*backed companies* **Teamshares** ($245M raised) and
  **Common Trust** ($2.6M) productize employee ownership — but they convert *other, mature/small* businesses,
  **not** startups. (Note: these are operating companies, not PE/VC funds. EO *funds* — Apis & Heritage,
  Mosaic — are buyout-style, mostly not classic VC.)
- **Operator bridge:** *"you don't need a unicorn / keep your equity / startup equity is broken"* essays are
  spreading (Gal Ratner; The Fund CFO; Dan Luu; Steve Blank).

## 7. Social-listening evidence (free stack: X + web + HN; Reddit/TikTok run-archived)

- **Employee sentiment = deep, persistent skepticism** (Hacker News, verbatim): *"I count startup stock and
  options as $0"* (#26098911); *"utterly worthless and frankly insulting to the workforce"* (#43677084);
  *"Equity in a startup for an employee is worthless"* (#16150463). Reddit (run #1): *"Run away, he is using

## 8. Why this matters for Post-Unicorn Finance

- **Empty space:** real worker ownership is a PE/succession story; **no VC has planted a flag on bringing it
  to startups.** Whether post-unicorn capital could be that vehicle is the natural next question — *posed, not
  claimed.*
- The **"equity is broken + liquidity migrating to secondaries"** reality is the same liquidity problem the
  atlas funds solve from the capital side.
- The **"keep your equity / don't chase a unicorn"** operator movement is the thesis surfacing in the
  worker-equity conversation.

## 11. 2026-06-19 delta — free-stack verification pass (ESOP / employee-participation lens)

Targeted free-stack pull (web + Exa + HN) on the two channels the prior run missed (rank-and-file sentiment;
the operator essay thread). **Every quote below was fetched from the source page, not a search snippet.**

**A. Employee OWNERSHIP is reaching tech — but as a mature-company succession / exit alternative, NOT
venture-stage adoption (this directly answers the question).** Fresh, dated 2026 examples, every one framed as
"chose this *instead of* selling to PE / being acquired":
- **Hustle** (peer-to-peer texting platform, SF) — employees **bought the company out of Social Capital**
  (Chamath Palihapitiya's holding co) and went **100% employee-owned** on its 10th anniversary (2026-05-19);
  senior-to-newest equity spread kept **<4-to-1**. The closest thing to a "tech startup" going employee-owned —
  but it is a ~10-year-old company *exiting a VC portfolio*, not a venture-stage startup.
  (morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260519639905 ; nonprofitpro.com, 2026-05-26)
- **Text-Em-All** — the **first 100% employee-owned SaaS company in the US via an EOT** (with Common Trust);
  it chose an EOT **over an ESOP it had spent months preparing**, because the ESOP "tied most employee rewards
  to departure rather than active participation." Inclusive profit-sharing for all employees.
  (common-trust.com/case-studies/text-em-all-employee-ownership-trust, updated 2026-06-08)
- **Meraki** (healthcare comms) → **ESOP** (2026-04-21, prnewswire). **Bee Window** (Indiana home-improvement)
  → **100% ESOP, explicitly "choosing legacy over private equity"** (2026-04-17, power100.io). **P. Terry's**
  (already in §4). All mature/profitable; none venture-stage.
- **Read:** the EO wave is real and *accelerating* (multiple fresh June-2026 deals), but the §4 structural
  pattern holds — **EO is a succession / exit alternative at mature private firms, now including a few tech/SaaS
  ones; it is not venture-stage startups baking in broad ownership.** Note the recurring **EOT-over-ESOP** choice
  in small/tech firms (ESOP's leave-triggered payouts + cash-buyout burden don't fit thin margins).

**B. Conventional startup-equity distrust is loud and current — but verify the viral claims.** A
high-engagement Hacker News thread (Jun 2026) around the **Nvidia–Groq deal** ran the line *"Groq is now
changing the deal after the fact by making those stock options worthless 100% of the time… that's theft"*
(news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46384192; companion thread …id=46381213). **Verification refuted the factual
claim:** the **~$20B Nvidia–Groq deal (May 28, 2026)** is reported to have **paid the cap table** — vested
equity in cash, unvested converted to vesting Nvidia stock, ~85% upfront (Yahoo Finance; TheStreet). So the
*distrust sentiment* is real and viral, but the *"options made worthless"* allegation is contested and likely
wrong. **Cite the sentiment, not the claim.**

**C. The operator "keep your equity / don't chase a unicorn" thread is real and growing — but it is FOUNDER
equity, not employee participation.** Verified June-2026 essays (quotes fetched from source):
- Keith Rumjahn, *"I raised $20M and lost the one thing I started a business for"* — *"the happiest I ever was
  as a founder was before anyone gave me money"* (rumjahn.substack.com, 2026-06-08).
- Dar Patel quoting investor Ruben Austin — *"a profitable company at five million in revenue, sold for three or
  four times, can return more to a founder who owns most of it than a hundred-million valuation where you own
  twenty percent"* (littlepursuits.substack.com, 2026-06-17).
- Todd Gagne / Wildfire Labs — *"Ownership beats valuation. Freedom beats fame. Control beats capital"*
  (wildfirelabs.substack.com, 2026-03-05).
- **Caveat (important):** this thread is about *founders* retaining ownership vs. dilution — a bridge to the
  post-unicorn / anti-power-law thesis, **not** evidence of broad *employee* value participation. Gagne's own
  example notes Jawbone's common shareholders, "including employees who'd traded years for equity, received
  nothing."
