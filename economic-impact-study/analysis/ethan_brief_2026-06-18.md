# Baseline Brief for Ethan — State of Value Participation (refreshed 2026-06-19)

**One-page decision brief.** Answers Ethan's question — *is meaningful employee value participation (ESOPs,
broad equity, profit-sharing) becoming a trend across startups, and where does the conversation sit in new
PE and VC?* — as a **neutral baseline to greenlight the next phase**, not a pitch. Backed by the consolidated
baseline (`state_of_value_participation.md` — the canonical living doc; dated source pulls archived under
`archive/baseline_sources_2026-06-19/`) and a verified, adversarial VC-dimension sweep (44 agents; 14
confirmed findings).

## Bottom line

There is **no single "yes, it's a trend."** Value participation is **three distinct movements** moving at
different speeds — conflating them produces wrong conclusions, so keep them separate:

1. **Conventional startup equity is distrusted and worth less** — declining, not rising.
2. **True employee *ownership* is genuinely accelerating — but as business-succession / PE / policy, NOT at
   venture-stage startups.**
3. **Employee *liquidity* is the clearest rising trend** — tenders and secondaries, not new ownership.

And on "where's the conversation": **PE leads it; VC is following-to-absent** (now verified, below).

## The three movements (kept distinct)

**1 — Conventional equity: broken in employees' eyes.** New-hire equity packages **−37%** (Nov 2022→Jan 2024,
Carta, verbatim); option-exercise rate fell **58%→30%** (Nov 2021→Nov 2023, Carta). Rank-and-file sentiment is
deeply skeptical and *longstanding*: "I count startup stock and options as $0" (Hacker News). Counter-signal:
AngelList says **63% of under-30s** would take less salary for more equity, up from 41% in 2021 — appetite for
upside is up even as trust in the instrument is down.

**2 — Employee ownership: a real, accelerating moment — outside startups.** ~**18% of US workers (~25M)** hold
some ownership stake; ~11M in ESOPs (Aspen Institute, 2026). DOL reports a **+28%** rise in leveraged
standalone ESOPs; co-ops up ~3.5× in a decade. The financing ecosystem grew "from a small handful of funds to
more than three dozen active managers" (ImpactAlpha, 2025). But the engine is **retiring-owner succession (the
"silver tsunami"), PE shared-ownership, and bipartisan policy** — e.g. P. Terry's (Austin, ~1,800 staff)
adopting an Employee Ownership Trust with profit-sharing rising 5%→20% (2026-06-09). It is **not** venture-stage
startups adopting broad ownership.

**3 — Liquidity: the clearest rising trend** (numbers attributed by source + period — do not blend):
- **Ethan's own example, now realized:** the **SpaceX IPO (June 12, 2026)** created **~4,400 employee
  millionaires** — among the largest corporate wealth-creation events on record.
- VC secondaries **$61.1B** vs VC-backed IPOs **$58.8B**, Jul 2024–Jun 2025 (Carta) — secondaries overtook IPOs.
- All US private-company secondaries **>$100B** in 2025 (Fabrica Ventures).
- Tender transaction value **$3.8B (2023) → $16.5B (2025)**, a record **16,538 employee participants** in 2025
  (Carta/Peter Walker); ~**$18.4B** in 2025 by PitchBook's count; **+68% YoY** in Q2 2025 (Carta).
- OpenAI's Oct-2025 tender let **600+ employees sell ~$6.6B at a $500B valuation** (up to $30M each) — recurring
  tenders are becoming a standing comp instrument at mature private firms (also Stripe, Anthropic).

## Where the conversation sits — PE leads, VC follows/absent (verified)

- **PE is leading** — Ownership Works (KKR's Pete Stavros): 184 companies, 268,000 non-C-suite employees,
  goal of **$20B for workers by 2030**. AInvest's "quiet takeoff" frames >$1.3B already distributed to 41,000
  non-exec employees since 2022.
- **Policy + public-company momentum (fresh):** Rhode Island's Senate **unanimously passed** a bill to create a
  State Employee Ownership Center (per @theNCEO, 2026-06-18); Mastercard launched a global **ESPP** for
  "broad-based employee participation" (Morgan Stanley at Work) — broad-equity expansion at a public company.
- **VC is following-to-absent on *broad* employee ownership** (adversarially verified across 6 angles; 14
  confirmed findings). **No VC fund was found whose stated thesis is broad employee ownership / "equity for
  all"** — there is no VC analog to Ownership Works. The most current VC voice (Bijan Sabet, Spark Capital,
  Apr 2026) treats fair employee equity as **table-stakes mechanics** — *"I don't think any of this is
  radical"* — i.e. doing options a bit more fairly, not a broad-ownership program. (Older, *longstanding*
  voices say the same: Hunter Walk, 2020, expected broad value-sharing to come from *"non-venture backed
  companies"*; Fred Wilson questioned broad grants in 2018.)
- **Where venture money DOES touch employee ownership, it's picks-and-shovels:** VCs fund startups that convert
  *other* (small, mature) businesses to employee ownership — **Teamshares** ($245M from QED/USV/Khosla) and
  **Common Trust** ($2.6M seed, Crossbeam/Schmidt Futures). That is a real but narrow category, orthogonal to
  broad ownership *at venture-backed startups themselves*. (Both are operating companies, not PE/VC funds.)
- **Academia is actively trying to pull VC in** (Rutgers' recurring "Silicon Valley Employee Ownership
  Symposium," founded on a gift from Carta's CEO; Aspen frames venture people who move into EO as "defectors").
- A small, diffuse **operator "equity is broken / employees deserve more"** essay thread exists (Alissa
  Orlando: *"every company… should offer equity to every employee"*; Ben Yoskovitz on employees 1-10).

## The open question (for Ethan to decide — NOT asserted here)

The baseline surfaces a structural gap: broad value participation is currently a **PE / mature-company**
story, and **no VC has claimed the "broad employee ownership at venture-stage companies" thesis.** Whether
**post-unicorn capital is positioned to be the vehicle that brings value participation to startups** is the
inference this baseline *sets up* — it is an **open question for Ethan**, deliberately not a claim of this brief.

## Confidence & caveats (binding)

- "VC is absent" means **"we ran 6 angles and found no analog to Ownership Works,"** not proven absence
  (searched-not-found; treat as moderate confidence).
- **Liquidity figures differ by scope/period by design** — each is attributed; do not collapse them into one
  number.
- Native engagement metrics are **partial**: X is now authenticated (operator/VC/policy posts captured, but
  engagement was modest), while Reddit/TikTok/IG via the paid scraper are **off** — so the sentiment read stays
  largely qualitative. The AngelList "63%" and Ravio "58% UK" stats are **single-vendor**; cite as attributed.
- The Sabet "not radical" quote shows VCs treat fair equity as *unremarkable / table-stakes* — it is **not**
  evidence of a deliberately "capped" ambition (his point is that fairness *should* be unremarkable).
