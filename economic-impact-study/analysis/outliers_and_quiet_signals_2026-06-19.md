# Outliers & Quiet Signals — broad employee value participation (2026-06-19)

**Purpose.** Not a trend census. A curated hunt for a *few* genuine **outliers**: startups/founders unusually
heading toward broad employee value participation (ESOP/EOT/profit-share/broad equity), and VCs/capital funds
**quietly signaling** a move that way. Pairs with the baseline `state_of_value_participation.md` (which found the
broad picture: ownership = mature/PE/succession, not startups; VC largely absent).

**Method.** Verified discovery workflow (`w62v7piiz`): ~12 finder agents fanned out globally → 46 raw → 42 unique
candidates → each **adversarially verified** (fetch the real source, confirm a verbatim quote, write the
strongest argument *against* counting it, default to skepticism). 55 agents total. Every quote below was
confirmed on its source page.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Yes — a small but real handful of startups and one notable VC firm are genuinely moving toward broad employee
value participation. But it is a thin, early, sparse trend, not an industry shift** — and most loud-sounding
examples are either (a) mature small-business successions (UK EOTs) or (b) *governance/mission* structures that
give workers **control or mission-lock but explicitly NOT economic upside.** The confidently-qualifying
venture-stage set is small, and that smallness is itself the finding.

---

## 2. Company outliers (verified case studies)

Four clear cases. Two are **venture-stage** (Column, Orbik); two are **bootstrapped/profitable** (Outseta, Kit).
All confirmed, all high-confidence.

### 2.1 Column — venture-stage tech (US, San Francisco) · confidence: HIGH
A profitable, nationally chartered developer bank that **re-engineered the equity instrument itself** to remove
the three frictions that normally gut option pools: it is **non-dilutive, exercise-free, and made annually
cash-liquid** via company-funded secondaries at market price. ~100% founder + ~60-person employee owned, funded
from profit rather than an investor preference stack; explicit "two constituents: customers and employees" stance.
> "We've structured our equity to put employees first, ahead of any outsiders or investors"
[column.com/blog/debugging-equity](https://column.com/blog/debugging-equity)
**Why outlier:** redesigns the *mechanics* so employees actually capture value, vs. the standard
70–80%-diluted, exercise-gated, 10–15-yr-illiquid option. **Limit:** self-published recruiting/PR post; per-head
sizing and 100% coverage asserted not itemized; liquidity partial/discretionary.

### 2.2 Orbik Cybersecurity — venture-stage tech (Spain, Basque Country) · confidence: HIGH
The clearest **"rejected the exit"** venture case: an industrial-cybersecurity startup (spun out of IKERLAN,
€1.8M round) that **converted from a limited company into a Mondragon worker cooperative in January 2025**,
making workers owner-members.
> "Now it is once again a pioneer, becoming the first technological start-up to transform itself into a
> cooperative… after closing a 1,8 million euro round of financing, Orbik has today completed its transformation
> into a co-operative"
[tulankide.com/en/orbik-cybersecurity…](https://www.tulankide.com/en/orbik-cybersecurity-the-first-technological-start-up-to-become-a-co-operative)
**Why outlier:** a genuine venture-stage tech startup choosing a co-op over a power-law exit; framed as a template
for a cooperative-tech ecosystem. **Limit:** a *mixed* co-op (institutional partners co-own), and an
institution-orchestrated (MONDRAGON/IKERLAN) conversion, not a spontaneous founder choice.

### 2.3 Outseta — bootstrapped SaaS startup (US, remote) · confidence: HIGH
Eight-year-old bootstrapped SaaS (no VC) that bakes ownership into founding DNA: **every team member earns equity
at the same rate and on the same terms as the founders, with a public cap table** (non-founder CTO holds 16.21%).
> "everybody has the opportunity to work as much or as little as they'd like to earn equity in our business—
> earning that equity at the same rate and on the same terms as our founders"
[outseta.com/posts/outsetas-choose-your-own-adventure-compensation-model](https://www.outseta.com/posts/outsetas-choose-your-own-adventure-compensation-model)
**Why outlier:** structurally-equal, transparent ownership — the opposite of a diluted option pool. **Limit:**
small (~6 named owners, prefers staying ~20 people); partly a labor-for-equity swap, so ownership self-selects.

### 2.4 Kit (formerly ConvertKit) — bootstrapped SaaS startup (US, remote) · confidence: HIGH
Bootstrapped email-marketing SaaS that **distributes 52% of company profit to its team** (~$9.8M over 10 years)
and offers **every** team member all four wealth components, with open internal books.
> "Those quadrants map to four things we offer every team member: salary, 401k match, profit sharing, and equity."
[nathanbarry.com/kits-compensation-model](https://nathanbarry.com/kits-compensation-model/)
**Why outlier:** majority-of-profit-to-team + equity for all, delivered partly as cash-now (not exit-only paper).
**Limit:** the headline mechanism is profit-*sharing* (cuttable, no governance/sale claim); rank-and-file equity
share unquantified vs. founder retention; founder-publicized (single source).

## 4. The big trap the verification caught — control ≠ value

Several impressive "employee-owned startup" candidates give workers **voting control or a mission-lock but
explicitly cap or eliminate economic upside** — so they are *contrasts* to broad employee VALUE participation,
not instances of it. Counting them would overstate the trend:
- **Sharetribe** (Finland): 19 of 21 became equal-voting stewards — but "the separation of money and power";
  upside is capped/concentrated.
- **Vyld**, **Wildplastic** (Germany), **Fresh Ventures** (NL), **Purpose Ventures/PEC**: steward-ownership —
  shares carry "voting rights but without dividend rights or participation in liquidation proceeds"; profit is
  mission-locked. Employees (and everyone) get **no economic upside by design**.

This is the same failure mode as the Groq rumor: the headline sounds like broad employee wealth-sharing; the
mechanism isn't.

## 5. Deliberately excluded (real, but off-angle)

Genuine broad ownership but **the exact pattern the study discounts** — mature small-business succession via the
UK EOT tax wave, or tiny services/co-op cases:
- **UK EOT successions:** Hallam, Searchability, The Software Bureau, Future Platforms (mature agencies, founder
  exit/CGT-relief driven).
- **Services / small co-ops:** Hiyield, Bocoup, Loomio, comp.coop, The Drivers Cooperative (small/contested).
- **Movement / infrastructure (not allocators):** Ownership Capital Lab, Exit-to-Community (Zebras Unite/MEDLab),
  Start.coop accelerator.
- **Roll-up / SMB-ETA (off-angle):** Obran (buys mature SMBs via LBO into worker ownership).
- **Early-employee parity, not broad:** Matterbeam (founder-grade equity for the first ~10 hires only).

## 6. Caveats (binding)

1. **Thin venture set.** Only a handful clear BOTH "broad employee value participation" AND "venture/startup-
   relevant." That sparseness is the finding, not a gap to pad.
2. **Control vs. value** (see §4) is the single biggest mislabel risk.
3. **Self-reported / single-source.** Column, Kit, Outseta rest largely on company/founder posts; Phoenix Court's
   claims are its own chairman's note (no Companies House filing pulled to confirm the foundation is registered
   top shareholder).
4. **Forward-rolling vs. executed.** Phoenix Court's all-FTE equity+carry runs "from 1H 2026 onwards" (only a
   first carry bonus confirmed); Index/Vestd are advocacy.
5. **Financier ≠ practitioner.** Acumen, Seed Commons, Equitable Economy Fund fund/promote ownership but don't
   broadly share value with their *own* staff; several are tiny pilots.
6. **Staleness:** MOSTLY AI's "phantom stock for all" (2022) — company since acquired; treat as historical.
   Some finder quotes were stitched/misattributed; only fragments confirmed on a real page were used.

## 7. Plain English

**Bottom line:** Yes, a small but real handful of startups and one notable VC firm are genuinely heading toward
sharing real ownership with all their workers — but it's a thin, early trend, and a lot of the loud examples
either are mature small-business sales or are setups that give workers a *vote* but not *money*.

**What the question means:** are companies/investors sharing real financial value (ownership, profit, equity
upside) with **all** workers, not just founders/execs? Common tools: an **ESOP** (US — employees collectively own
shares via a trust), an **EOT** (UK equivalent), or **profit-sharing** (cash slice of profits to everyone).

**Startup side — the real ones:** **Outseta** (everyone earns equity on the same terms as founders, public cap
table proving it), **Column** (a bank that redesigned employee equity so it's non-dilutive, free to exercise, and
cashable every year), **Kit/ConvertKit** (every employee gets salary + retirement match + profit-sharing +
equity, and 52% of profit goes to the team), and **Orbik** — a venture-stage Spanish cybersecurity startup that
turned itself into a worker cooperative instead of chasing a big sale.

**VC side — the headline:** **Phoenix Court** (formerly LocalGlobe) is restructuring *itself* so every full-time
employee — not just senior partners — gets equity, carry, and profit share. That's rare: a VC doing to itself
what it tells its startups to do. Beyond that, **Index Ventures** publicly tells its startups to give every
employee options (advice, not its own action), and a few small impact/co-op funds (**Equitable Economy Fund**,
**nth Venture**, **Acumen→Obran**, **Seed Commons**) *fund* employee-owned companies rather than practice it
themselves.

**What to watch out for:** several "employee-owned" startups (Sharetribe, Vyld, the Purpose-network funds) give
workers *voting control* or lock profits to a mission — but **deliberately give employees no financial upside**.
Impressive, but not the same thing.

**So:** the movement is real and worth watching, but small. Genuine, executed, broad employee *value*
participation at venture-stage tech companies is still the exception — championed by a few founders and one VC
firm — not yet an industry shift.
